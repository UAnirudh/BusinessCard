# Tappd business validation plan

## Positioning to test

Start with individuals who meet prospective clients or collaborators often. Keep creating, opening, and saving a public card free. Test whether a reliable follow-up workflow—capturing who someone is, where you met, notes, reminders, and export—is valuable enough to pay for. Do not treat a price survey or competitor price as proof of demand.

The category is free-first and crowded. Blinq lists a free tier and Premium at $9.99 monthly or $7.33 per month billed annually; HiHello lists Personal free and Professional at $6 per month billed annually; Wave lists a free individual tier with wallet, QR, and NFC sharing. This makes card creation and basic sharing a weak paid differentiator. Recheck the live pages before setting Tappd pricing:

- [Blinq pricing](https://blinq.me/pricing)
- [HiHello pricing](https://www.hihello.com/pricing)
- [Wave individual plans](https://wavecnct.com/individuals)

## First 10 interviews

Recruit a balanced sample because the founder has not selected a niche: four independent consultants/freelancers, three recruiters, and three real-estate agents. Screen for people who meet at least two new professional contacts per month. Use more than one Seattle channel, and keep these cohort counts even if one organization produces most of the replies. Young Professionals of Seattle can help reach early-career professionals, but do not let that channel silently replace the three role-based quotas. These are discovery cohorts, not a conclusion about which group will pay.

Ask about their last three introductions, how each person’s details were captured, what happened next, what was forgotten, and the exact consequences. Ask them to demonstrate their current workflow if they are comfortable. Only after hearing their story, show the public Tappd card and contact-saving flow. Invite participants into a two-week pilot and observe real use.

Use these directional gates to decide which cohort to test next:

- At least six of ten describe a recent, concrete follow-up failure or costly manual workaround.
- At least four agree to a two-week pilot; at least three use Tappd after a real introduction.
- Continue only if people return after the initial card setup and use the follow-up feature in real situations.
- After repeated use, test a specific offer such as $5.99/month or $49.99/year against free. These are hypotheses, not validated prices or a profit forecast.

At a hypothetical $5.99 monthly price, 15% store commission leaves about $5.09 before taxes, refunds, support, infrastructure, and acquisition costs. At $49.99 annually it leaves about $42.49 per year on the same commission assumption. Actual store fees depend on eligibility and program rules; check them before building a forecast.

### Interview guide

1. Walk me through your last three new professional introductions.
2. How did you exchange and store each person's details?
3. What happened after you met? When did you follow up?
4. Was anything lost, forgotten, or delayed? Tell me about one specific time.
5. What did that cost you or the other person?
6. What do you use now? What do you do when that process fails?
7. How many introductions like this do you have in a typical month?
8. If useful, show me what you do today.
9. Let the participant try a card link, contact save, and follow-up reminder. Observe where they hesitate; do not explain the interface until they ask.
10. Invite them to use it after real introductions for two weeks. Ask what they expect to do next time; measure what they actually do.

Avoid leading questions such as “Would you use this?” Ask about past behavior and watch real use instead.

## Wallet and no-app sharing

Anyone can already open `/c/{slug}` in a browser without installing Tappd, scan its QR with a standard camera, and save the public contact as a vCard. Make this the default “works for everyone” path. A vCard download is a contacts import file, not an Apple Wallet or Google Wallet pass.

True Wallet passes are separate from the no-app vCard path. This repo now has Apple `.pkpass` and Google Wallet web-issuance handlers, but their buttons stay hidden until issuer configuration is complete. Apple requires a signed pass tied to a Pass Type ID certificate; this implementation uses OpenSSL to sign the pass manifest. Google requires an issuer, an approved Generic Pass class, and a server-signed JWT; a new Google issuer starts in demo mode and can issue only to test users until publishing access is granted. Keep signing keys server-side, configure the server OpenSSL binary, and verify both passes on physical devices before advertising this as launch-ready.

- [Apple: build a Wallet pass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/building-a-pass)
- [Apple: distribute a pass from a website or app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/distributing-and-updating-a-pass)
- [Google Wallet: issue passes from a website](https://developers.google.com/wallet/generic/web)
- [Google Wallet: issuer onboarding](https://developers.google.com/wallet/generic/getting-started/issuer-onboarding)

## Brand and business risk

An existing digital-card business uses the Tappd name at [tappd.co.za](https://tappd.co.za/pages/pricing). Before spending on paid acquisition or final brand assets, check trademark availability, app-store name conflicts, and whether the domain/brand could confuse users. This is a diligence item, not a legal conclusion.

## Store economics and launch gate

Store fees, payment rules, and enrollment requirements change. Before launch, verify the current [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) and [Google Play payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en). Digital in-app features may require store billing. Keep this pricing experiment out of production until there is repeat usage and a compliant purchase flow.

This plan does not claim profitability. Profitability depends on retained paid users, store fees, service costs, support, and customer acquisition, none of which have been measured yet.
