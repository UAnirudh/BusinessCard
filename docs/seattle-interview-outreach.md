## Interview outline

1. Tell me about the last time you exchanged professional contact details.
2. How did you share your information, and what did the other person do with it?
3. What do you use today to keep track of people you meet?
4. When do you usually forget to follow up, or lose context about a contact?
5. Have you tried a digital business card or contact-management app? What made you keep or stop using it?
6. How important is it that a recipient can save your details without installing an app?
7. What would make you trust or distrust a shareable card that saves to a phone's contacts or Wallet?
8. If you could change one part of your current process, what would you change?

Ask about the most recent real example before describing Tappd. Record the interview only with the participant's permission. Track answers without collecting contact lists or sensitive personal information.

## What to measure

- Recruit 10 interviews: roughly 4 consultants/advisors, 3 recruiters, and 3 real-estate professionals or other network-heavy solo professionals.
- Count repeated problems in sharing, saving, remembering context, and follow-up.
- Note current alternatives and what people already pay for; don't infer willingness to pay from compliments.
- After the interview, ask whether they want to try a prototype and what real event they would use it for.
- Continue with a segment only if at least 5 of 10 describe the same recent problem unprompted and at least 3 agree to try the workflow in a real introduction.

## Product wording until Wallet issuance is configured

The current public-card path works in a browser without Tappd and offers a vCard contact download. Apple Wallet and Google Wallet passes require issuer/signing credentials and approvals, which are not configured yet. Describe that feature as planned or in setup; do not promise that it is available until real passes have been issued and verified on devices.
